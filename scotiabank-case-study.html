Context

Six markets, each with its own banking systems, regulations and ways of working, delivered through one platform to corporate clients.

Workflows, terminology, onboarding and guidance were inconsistent across the file-based services clients use to send payments and data. Every inconsistency became something a client had to learn, and something four teams had to maintain.

03

The system

Whatever the market or channel, every file a client sends goes through the same chain. Standardizing meant treating it as one chain rather than four separate services.

04

Key decisions

One model for four file services

Problem
Workflows, terminology, onboarding and guidance differed from service to service and market to market.
Decision
Define H2H, H2W, File Transfer and File Upload against one shared file lifecycle and one vocabulary, with market differences handled as variations of it.
Why
A client using two services, or a team building one, shouldn't need two mental models.
Impact
One reference model for four product teams to design and build against.

Benchmark before standardizing

Problem
Without an outside reference, every existing workflow looked like a requirement.
Decision
Benchmark file and bulk-payment workflows across 17 banking and payments platforms before defining the standard.
Why
Patterns that recur across the market show what clients already expect, and where a local difference is a real constraint.
Impact
Recurring workflow patterns, a platform-landscape framework and an executive conclusion to anchor decisions.

Four phases instead of nine steps

Problem
A granular nine-step design process was hard to run the same way across four teams.
Decision
Consolidate it into a reusable playbook with four phases: Scouting, Planning, Delivery and Learning.
Why
Fewer, broader phases are easier to adopt and to govern, without losing the checkpoints that matter.
Impact
A shared delivery framework, connected to the integrated-services work.

Reflection

Standardizing across six markets was a language problem before it was an interface problem. Once the services shared a vocabulary and a lifecycle, the screens were the easy part.

It's the work I'm most useful for: finding the structure inside something complicated, and making it the thing every team builds from.
